Chords and fretboard
Open chords
Pick a chord to see every position across the neck, with each note your fingers are pressing.
7th chords add the 7th note of the scale to a triad. They add emotion and colour — maj7 sounds warm, m7 sounds mellow, dominant 7 sounds bluesy.
Sus chords replace the 3rd with the 2nd or 4th. They sound unresolved — neither major nor minor. Great for adding movement and colour.
Extended chords add the 9th or 6th for extra colour. Gadd9 and Cadd9 are especially common in pop and rock — they sound bigger and richer than basic chords.
Slash chords (G/B, C/E) play a chord with a different bass note. They create smooth walking basslines between chords — the hallmark of intermediate playing.
Triads use only 3 notes on strings 1-3. Learn all 3 inversions of major and minor triads — they unlock a whole new way to comp and solo anywhere on the neck.

5 Tests
Jaxon Williams · Am I intermediate?
Pass all 5 tests (+ the bonus) and you're playing at an intermediate level. Tap each test to see what to practise.
1
CAGED — find any chord across the neck
›
Start with E and A shape barre chords, then use the CAGED system to find the same chord in all 5 positions up the neck. Every chord has 5 shapes — can you find them all?
Lock in your E shape barre chord (e.g. F at fret 1, G at fret 3)
Lock in your A shape barre chord (e.g. B at fret 2, C at fret 3)
Pick any chord (e.g. G) and find all 5 CAGED shapes up the neck
CAGED shapes for G: G shape (open), E shape (fret 3), D shape (fret 5), C shape (fret 7), A shape (fret 10)
2
Chord colour — maj7 and m7 shapes
›
Upgrade basic chords with colour. C → Cmaj7, Am → Am7. Learn the 4 movable shapes (E maj7, E m7, A maj7, A m7) so you can add colour in any key.
Cmaj7: lift your index finger off the open C chord — that's the maj7
Am7: lift your ring finger off Am — simple and sounds great
E shape maj7 barre: play an E shape barre and remove the index on the G string
A shape maj7 barre: A shape barre — add the maj7 on the B string (one fret below the barre)
E shape m7 barre: standard minor barre, remove the index on the B string
A shape m7 barre: minor A shape barre, remove the index on the B string
3
Chord decoration — hammers, sus, fills
›
Make chords come alive. Instead of just strumming, hammer into them. Add sus chords for movement. Sneak a little riff inside a progression without losing the rhythm.
Chord hammers: strum the chord open, then hammer your fingers down — the attack changes completely
Dsus2 (xx0230) → D (xx0232) → Dsus4 (xx0233): practice rolling through these in one motion
Take a G-C-D progression and decorate just one chord per run-through without breaking rhythm
Start point: nail the hammer-on technique first, then add decoration one chord at a time
4
Triads — strings 1–3 only
›
Play a G-C-D progression as triads on strings 1-3 only. Major and minor triads in three positions — and relate each shape back to a CAGED shape you already know.
Major triad shapes on strings 1-3 (high e, B, G): Root position, 1st inversion, 2nd inversion
Minor triad shapes on strings 1-3: same three inversions, flatten the 3rd
Play G-C-D as triads staying in one area of the neck — don't jump around
Link each triad to its CAGED parent shape: it's just the top 3 strings of the barre chord
G major triads (strings 1-3):
Root position: e=G(3rd fret) B=D(3rd) G=G(open) · frets 0-3
1st inversion: e=B(7th fret) B=G(8th) G=D(7th) · frets 7-8
2nd inversion: e=D(10th fret) B=B(12th) G=G(12th) · frets 10-12
5
Chord connections — inversions & slash chords
›
Beginners play chords as blocks. Intermediates use inversions and slash chords to create smooth bass movement between chords.
Beginner: C → Am → F → G
Intermediate: C → G/B → Am → C/E → F → D/F# → G

The bass note walks down: C → B → A → (E) → F → F# → G. That movement is what creates the connection.
G/B: G chord with B in the bass (5th string, 2nd fret only, mute low E)
C/E: C chord with E in the bass (6th string open or 4th string 2nd fret)
D/F#: D chord with F# in the bass (6th string 2nd fret)
Start point: learn chord inversions — same chord, different bass note, different feel
